N.B signals are real.
the random process->remove the mean (its not time varying).

compute fourier transform:

y~(f) = int_-infty^infty exp(i 2pi f t) y(t) dt
y(t) = int_-infty^infty exp(-i 2pi f t) y~(f) df

Parsevals theorem: 

int_-infty^infty (y(t))^2 = int_-infty^infty |y~(f)|^2 
as y(t) is real, y~(f) = - y~(-f)
we can fold the frequencies
2*int_0^infty |y~(f)|^2 = int_-infty^infty |y~(f)|^2 

N.B not useful for random process (as the fourier transform would diverge), talk about timescales that are long compared to the frequency of interest.
i.e. we look at the laser power over an hour. truncate signal, y_T(t):

y_T(t) = 0 for t >  |T/2| or y(t) < |T/2| 

int_-infty^infty (y_T(t))^2 = 2*int_0^infty |y_T~(f)|^2

this is proportional to T. Need to normalise by diving by T.

1/T int_-infty^infty (y_T(t))^2 = 2/T*int_0^infty |y_T~(f)|^2

the LHS is the means. Mean of y vanishes (as it was chosen), so we get the mean square of the oscillation on the LHS.
RHS tells you what noise at various frequencies you have.

define the RHS quantity that is integrated over -> 2/T |int_-T/2^T/2 exp(i 2pi f t) y(t) dt|^2 = S_y(f)

S_y(f) is the spectral density of the signal y_T
by parsevals, int_0^infty S_y = means square(y).
This is a power/Hz.
over a frequency band, the rms noise is
delta y = sqrt(S_y(f) delta f)

any linear process can be written as a filter, and by convolution theorem we can study how spectral density changes with filtering.

each photon turns to an electron: so,

I(t) consists of pulses caused by photon->electron with duration tau with arival time t_j. tau is small compared to 1/grav wave frequency.

fourier transform of the pulse is long compared t, dies out at 1/tau pulse. Huge compared to grav wave f.

I(t) = sum_j I_j(t-tj)
first truncate 
I(t) -> I_T(t)
take fourier transform

|I_T~(f)|^2 = sum_jk I_j~(f) exp(i2piftj) I_k~(f) exp(-i2piftk)
different pulses are not correlated, all product where j not k cancel.

|I_T~(f)|^2 = sum_j |I_j~(f)|^2 
as we are interest at low frequencies near 0.
|I_T~(f)|^2 = sum_j |I_j~(0)|^2 

I_j~(0) = int(I_y exp(i2pift)dt = energy of one pulse = hbar omega_e
so the sum is just adding the pulses
|I_T~(f)|^2 = sum_j |I_j~(0)|^2  = N (hbar omega_e)^2

spectral density -> S_i = 2/T * (N hbar omega_e)^2 , 2/T* N = twice the rate of incoming photons = R
S_i = 2 R (hbar omega_e)^2
	= 2 I_0/hbar_omega_e (hbar omega_e)^2
	= 2I_0 hbar omega_e
standard result for quantised shots of energy where the pulse duration is short compared to the measurement time.
