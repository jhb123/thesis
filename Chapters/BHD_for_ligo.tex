A major change between aLIGO and LIGO A+ will be the replacement of DC readout (see Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_alternatives}) with balanced homodyne detection (BHD). It is explained why BHD forms a key part of many quantum optics experiments (e.g.~\cite{Walls1983,Yu2020,Stefszky2012}) in Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_what_is_bhd}. Additionally, BHD is required for more complex topologies of interferometer \cite{Knyazev2018,Eberle2010,Kimble2001,Zhang2018}.
\paragraph{}
The motivations for implementing BHD into the LIGO detectors are described in Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_what_is_bhd}. This can be summarised as: if the detectors are to reach their design sensitivity, they require BHD. Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochaptersketchoflodelivery} is a sketch of the local oscillators beam path. In contrast to DC readout, the local oscillator (LO) and signal do not follow the same optical path; the consequence of this is the local oscillator path length must be stable enough to not contaminate the signal with noise. This is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_LO_stability}. The proposed BHD layout is discussed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:BHD_for_ligo_2}.

\section{What is Balanced Homodyne Detection?}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_what_is_bhd}
%By combining a signal beam with a much stronger LO beam, phase modulations in the signal beam become amplitude modulations in the combined LO-signal beam (e.g.~\cite{Danilishin2012}). BHD can be configured to measure any quadrature of the signal beam which is vital for inferring the quantum state of a system, and is required for more complex topologies of interferometer \cite{Knyazev2018,Eberle2010,Kimble2001,Zhang2018}. BHD has been used to detect 11.6\,dB of shot noise suppression to below \SI{1}{\hertz} due to the squeezing of vacuum fluctuations. \cite{Stefszky2012}.
%\paragraph{}
BHD is a method of measuring the information encoded in any quadrature of a signal beam (see e.g.~\cite[Section 2]{Danilishin2012}). The signal beam is set up to interfere with a LO beam with the same optical frequency. A sketch of a BHD readout is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterbhdsketch}. BHD is implemented by injecting the LO and signal beams into different input ports of a 50:50 beamsplitter and by measuring the interference with photodiodes at the beamsplitter's output ports. The information encoded in an arbitrary quadrature of the signal beam can be selected by correctly setting the relative phase between the LO and signal beams. The relative phase between the LO and signal beam is also known as the \emph{homodyne angle}. Subtracting the two photocurrents results in a measurement which is proportional to the selected quadrature of the signal beam (see Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_QM_BHD}). Additionally, the subtraction results in the removal of the classical intensity noise of the LO from the measurement. 
\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{Figures/BHD_for_ligo_chapter_BHD_sketch}
	\caption{A coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$, enters port $a$ of a beam splitter. A signal $\ket{\Psi}$ enters port $b$. These two beams interfere, resulting in photons being detected at the $c$ and $d$ ports. The two photocurrents are subtracted, and the signal that is produced is proportional to an arbitrary quadrature of the state $\ket{\Psi}$; the difference in the signals at the $c$ and $d$ ports is given by Equation (\ref{eq:bhd_signal}).} The quadrature of the state $\ket{\Psi}$ that is observed depends on the homodyne angle, $\phi$. 
	\label{fig:bhdforligochapterbhdsketch}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{}
Without BHD, the LIGO detectors will not be able to reach the design sensitivity for A+. A squeezed light field can be used to surpass the standard quantum limit (see Chapter \ref{chapter:Gravitational_wave_interferometers}). To reach the level of frequency dependent squeezing needed to meet the A+ design sensitivity, the homodyne angle must be \ang{90}. There is a low amount of tolerance on the homodyne angle before the quantum noise is significantly degraded \cite{eric_oelker_2021}, and the current readout method (see Section~\ref{sec:BHD_for_ligo_alternatives}) does not offer sufficient control over the homodyne angle. Currently, the squeezing angle is set to be at the same angle as the readout quadrature; the preference would be to set the readout quadrature to the squeezing angle~\cite{Tse2019}. The effect that controlling the squeezing angle has on the quantum noise floor has been demonstrated at LLO \cite{Yu2020}. However, \cite{Yu2020} used a diagnostic BHD setup which was too noisy to make gravitational wave detections. 
\paragraph{}
%\paragraph{}
%Ideally the LO would have no phase noise, however in reality there are fluctuations in the phase between the LO and the signal beam. The interference of the LO and signal beam causes this phase noise to become an amplitude modulation in the combined LO-signal beam\cite{Steinlechner2015}. This phase noise must be kept below the size of the phase fluctuations in the signal beam that are to be measured, which in A+ are determined by the shot noise of the light within the arm cavities. Unlike Heterodyne readout, BHD does not cause a fundamental increase in the noise floor of the measurement.
\subsection{Alternatives to BHD}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_alternatives}
The current detection scheme is known as DC readout~\cite{Fricke2012}; this is a form of homodyne detection. A differential offset in the arm lengths produces the LO in DC readout; the size of the LO can be set with the differential offset in the length of the arms, and the homodyne angle can be set with the differential arm power~\cite{Fritschel2014}. DC readout has some key positive outcomes, such as there being no phase noise between the LO and the signal, and the LO and signal being perfectly aligned and mode matched. However, while theoretically the homodyne angle can be set, due to different amounts of loss in each arm, the homodyne angle is not a free parameter; for LLO, the homodyne angle during O3 was $\sim\ang{11}$~\cite{BHD_angle_2020}. The inability to tune the homodyne angle increased the level of the detector's quantum noise \footnote{This increase was buried under the classical noise floor; however, the LIGO detectors will be quantum noise limited in the future}. 
\paragraph{}
The first generation of detectors used heterodyne readout (e.g.~\cite{Sigg2008}). Heterodyne readout involves a modulation-demodulation scheme whereby the signal is beat with a radio-frequency (RF) LO to a frequency where technical noise is no longer a concern. It is then demodulated at the same modulation frequency to recover the original signal. This method comes with the draw back that the shot noise is enhanced by the modulation-demodulation process \cite{Meers1991}. Heterodyne detection is described in more detail in the final paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:pd_chapter_grav_wave_ifo_requirments}.
\subsection{Quantum Mechanical Description of BHD}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_QM_BHD}
To understand how BHD can be used to detect quantum states of light, it is useful to mathematically express light and interference in terms of quantum mechanics. From the two-photon formalism of light (see Section \ref{sec:detector_chapter_two_photon_formalism}), the general quadrature operator, $\mathbf{\hat{X}}_\phi$ can be obtained,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\hat{X}}_\phi = \mathbf{\hat{X}}\cos\phi + \mathbf{\hat{Y}}\sin\phi,
\label{eq:BHD_for_ligo_general_quadrature}
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ is the homodyne angle. By setting the homodyne angle, one can sense an arbitrary combination of the phase and amplitude of a field.
%\begin{equation}
%\alpha_{\rm{LO}} = \left| \alpha_{\rm{LO}} \right|\exp^{i\phi}.
%\end{equation}
\paragraph{}
Consider the interference of a LO field, $\hat{a}$, and a signal field, $\hat{b}$, on a 50:50 beamsplitter. The resulting fields,  $\hat{c}$ and $\hat{d}$, are detected on two photodiodes. This is shown in Figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterbhdsketch}. The photocurrents are proportional to 
\begin{align}
\mathbf{\hat{N}}_c = \hat{c}^\dagger\hat{c} &= \left(\sqrt{R}\hat{a}^\dagger +\sqrt{T}\hat{b}^\dagger\right) \left(\sqrt{R}\hat{a} +\sqrt{T}\hat{b}\right) \\
&= R\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + T\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} + \sqrt{RT}(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{b} + \hat{b}^\dagger\hat{a})\\
\mathbf{\hat{N}}_d = \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d} &= T\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + R\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} - \sqrt{RT}(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{b} + \hat{b}^\dagger\hat{a}).
\end{align}
As the beam splitter is 50:50, $R = T = 1/2$. This results in the difference in the photocurrents being proportional to
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\hat{N}}_c - \mathbf{\hat{N}}_d = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{b} + \hat{b}^\dagger\hat{a}.
\label{eq:bhd_photonnumber}
\end{equation}
The operators $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{b}$ can be `linearised', $ \hat{a}\rightarrow \alpha + \delta a$ in order to separate the quantum behaviour and classical behaviour. Furthermore, the phase between the signal field and the LO field can be expressed in terms of the homodyne angle with $\hat{b}\rightarrow \hat{b}\exp(i\phi)$. By neglecting terms of the order $\delta a \delta b$, this allows one to rewrite equation (\ref{eq:bhd_photonnumber}) as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\hat{N}}_c - \mathbf{\hat{N}}_d = 2\alpha\beta\cos\phi + 2\alpha\delta \mathbf{X}^b_{-\phi} + 2\beta\delta \mathbf{X}^a_\phi.
\end{equation}
The variance, $\sqrt{V(X)} = \Delta X$, of this measurement would then be
\begin{equation}
V( \mathbf{\hat{N}}_c - \mathbf{\hat{N}}_d) = 4\alpha^2 V(\delta \mathbf{X}^b_{-\phi}) + 4\beta^2 V(\delta \mathbf{X}^a_{\phi}).
\label{eq:BHD_var}
\end{equation}
If the LO is much stronger than the signal field, $\alpha \gg \beta$, then the measurement is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\hat{N}}_c - \mathbf{\hat{N}}_d = 2\alpha\beta\cos\phi + 2\alpha\delta \mathbf{X}^b_{-\phi}.
\label{eq:bhd_signal}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Other Motivations for Implementing BHD in LIGO A+}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_BHD_alt_motivation}

To optimise the sensitivity of the interferometer to different sources the detectors noise would need shaping, a task that can be achieved, along with other upgrades (e.g~\cite{Kimble2001,Zhang2020}), BHD.

\section{The Local Oscillator Path Length Stability Requirement for A+}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_LO_stability}
%In this section, a semi-classical approach is used to derive the path length stability requirement for the local oscillator. 
%If a full quantum mechanical treatment were used (e.g.~\cite{Steinlechner2015}), the result would be the same because the quantum mechanics of this problem are included in the A+ sensitivity curve and the expected power in the signal beam i.e.~since the LIGO interferometers are linear, their noise is transformed linearly, and the quantum nature of the noise is irrelevant to the calculation. The phase noise of the LO must be kept low enough so that it does not result in intensity fluctuations greater than those due to the shot noise of the light in the signal beam. This requirement was also derived in \cite{Zhang2019,Fritschel2017b} and \cite{HRTSrequirement}; however, in this section, the most current layout was considered. 
\paragraph{}
Phase noise arises due to phase fluctuations in the LO beam being indistinguishable from phase fluctuations in the signal beam. A gravitational wave produces a phase modulated signal in the signal beam, and the LO field phase is modulated by the motion of the mirrors in the LO path. When these beams are combined, it is the phase difference between the two fields that produces a signal, so the phase noise of the LO path must be low enough for the signal generated by the interference of the LO and signal beams to be due to the phase modulation of the signal beam. 
\paragraph{}
This section is structured as follows: first, the LO path will be described. Then, a linear response function between a differential strain and signal at the AS port will be obtained. Next, the sensitivity curve for A+ (see Figure~\ref{fig:detectorchapterdesignsensitivitycurves}) will be expressed in units of $\frac{\rm{W}}{\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}}$ by deconvolving it with the linear response function. This light noise curve was then used to find the maximum allowable intensity fluctuations due to phase noise between the LO and the signal beams. Finally, this phase noise requirement will be converted into a optical path length requirement.
\subsection{Local Oscillator Path}
The layout for the optics which combine the LO and signal beam is shown in Figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochaptersketchoflodelivery}. The LO will be picked off from the power recycling cavity and guided by one steering mirror, BHDM1, to the BHD combining beam splitter, BHDBS2. The signal beam will exit the OFI and be steered by one mirror, OM0, onto BHDBS2. OM0 and BHDM1 will be used to align the beams so they overlap on BHDBS2. The angle of BHDBS2 will be used to make the LO and signal beams overlap in the far field. Two lenses are used for mode matching the LO beam to the signal beam.
\paragraph{}
The LO could have been derived from a separate laser, but to acquire laser light with low frequency and intensity noise the LO will be picked off from a point within the interferometer. The two ways of obtaining a LO beam considered were a beam picked off from the main beam splitter's AR coating (BSAR) or the beam transmitted through PR2 (POP)~\cite{Zhang2019}. The determining factor in choice of pick-off point was the effect of backscatter. The POP port was chosen as it is far superior to the BSAR choice at all frequencies and scatter phases. This is because backscattered light from the BSAR enters only one arm, thus making it a differential affect. Any backscatter that enters the POP port is common-mode, and therefore DARM is far less sensitive to this source of backscatter.

	\caption{Sketch of the LO (green) and signal (pink) beam paths in relation to other parts of the interferometer (see Figure~\ref{fig:ligolayout} and Section~\ref{sec:detector_chapter_LIGO_layout}). The LO beam emerges from PR2. Some of this light is used to control the interferometer, so BHDBS1 is needed to split the light. BHDM1 directs the LO onto BHDBS2. The signal beam emerges from the SRM. \subsection{Calculation of the Local Oscillator Path Length Stability Requirement}
The first step is to express the A+ sensitivity curve in units of $\si{\watt/\sqrt{Hz}}$. The signal generated by the differential motion of the ETMs is low-passed by the coupled SRC-arm cavity, so the linear response function can be written as 
\begin{equation}
H(f) = \frac{H_0}{1 - if/f_c},
\label{eq:bhdforligochapter_linear_response_fn}
\end{equation}
where $H_0$ is a gain factor, $f$ is the frequency and $f_c$ is the corner frequency of the SRC-arm cavity. For the LIGO detectors, $f_c = 380\,\si{\hertz}$~\cite{Izumi2016}. This linear response function does not include optical spring effects, but these are small and not important for this calculation. $H_0$ can be calculated by dividing the expected level of shot noise, $s_{\rm{A+}}$, by the product of the A+ sensitivity curve (see Figure~\ref{fig:detectorchapterdesignsensitivitycurves}) and $1/(1 - if/f_c) $ at a frequency where the detector will be shot noise limited. The level of shot noise, in units of $\si{\watt/\sqrt{\hertz}}$, is given by
\begin{equation}
	s_{\rm{A+}} = \sqrt{2h\nu P}\times 10^{-\xi/20},
\end{equation}
where $h$ is Planck's constant, $\nu$ is the frequency of the light, $P = P_{\rm{LO}} + P_{\rm{sig}}$ is the power of the light that is being detected and $\xi$ is the amount of squeezing in decibels. For A+, $P_{\rm{LO}} = 100\rm{mW}$, $P_{\rm{sig}} = 5\rm{mW}$ and $\xi = 12\,\rm{dB}$. The linear response function is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterdetectorrepsonse}. To express the A+ sensitivity curve in units of $\si{\watt/\sqrt{Hz}}$, the sensitivity curve should be divided by the linear response function shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterdetectorrepsonse}.

	\label{fig:bhdforligochapterdetectorrepsonse}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{}
The next step is to use the equations for interference at a beamsplitter to convert a change in power into a change in phase. When the LO and signal beams interfere at BHSBS2, the exiting beams have power $P_c$ and $P_d$. These are given by
\begin{equation}
P_c = \left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}E_s e^{i\omega t} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\rm{LO}} e^{i\omega t + \phi}  \right|^2 = \frac{|E_s|^2}{2} + \frac{|E_{\rm{LO}}|^2}{2} + |E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\cos(\phi)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
P_d = \left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}E_s e^{i\omega t} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\rm{LO}} e^{i\omega t + \phi}  \right|^2 = \frac{|E_s|^2}{2} + \frac{|E_{\rm{LO}}|^2}{2} - |E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\cos(\phi),
\end{equation}
where $E_s$ and $E_{\rm{LO}}$ are the complex field amplitudes of the signal and LO beams; $\omega$ is the laser frequency; $t$ is time and $\phi$ is the homodyne angle. The difference in $P_c$ and $P_d$ signals gives the BHD signal,
\begin{equation}
P = 2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\cos(\phi).
\label{eq:classical_bhd}
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $\phi$ gives the relationship between a change in power and a change in phase between the two beams,
\begin{equation}
dP = -2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\sin(\phi) d\phi.
\end{equation}
Since the homodyne angle is 90\si{\degree}, this becomes
\begin{equation}
dP = -2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|d\phi.
\label{eq:bhdforligo_interference_at_a_beam_splitter}
\end{equation}
Using Equation~(\ref{eq:bhdforligo_interference_at_a_beam_splitter}), the A+ sensitivity curve can be expressed in units of $\si{\radian_{\rm{rms}}/\sqrt{Hz}}$. This is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterphasenoise}. This requirement was also derived in \cite{Zhang2019,Fritschel2017b} and \cite{HRTSrequirement}; however, these calculations all differ from each other by factors of $\sim1$. The result in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterphasenoise} is more pessimistic than the results in \cite{Zhang2019,Fritschel2017b} and \cite{HRTSrequirement} as a signal beam of 5\,mW was assumed.

	\caption{The sensitivity curve for the A+ detectors expressed in units of $\si{\radian_{\rm{rms}}/\sqrt{Hz}}$. At frequencies above 1\,kHz the noise is flat as it is dominated by shot noise. The red line shows the level of shot noise; this is $1.1\times10^{-9}\si{\radian_{\rm{rms}}/\sqrt{\hertz}}$. \paragraph{}
To express phase in terms of optical path length, the phase needs to be multiplied by $\lambda_{\rm{EM}}/(2\pi)$. The relationship between the optical path length, $n_p$, and the motion requirement for the optics, $n_o$, assumes  that there will be two mirrors at \ang{45} which are used to steer the AS and LO beams. As the optics reflect at 45\si{\degree}, the path length change due to the optic's motion is multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$. The beams are combined on a beam splitter, and the motion of this beam splitter needs to be counted twice. Figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterbhddoublecounted} illustrates this. The relationship between optical path length and the motion of the optics is $10\times\sqrt{(\sqrt{2}n_0)^2 + (\sqrt{2}n_0)^2 +2\times (\sqrt{2}n_0)^2}  = n_p$. Since there are many uncorrelated sources of noise in the LIGO detectors, a factor of 10 is used as a safety margin. Multiplying the phase noise curve shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterphasenoise} by $\lambda_{\rm{EM}}/(2\pi\times10\sqrt{8})$ gives the requirement for the longitudinal motion of the optics; this is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bhdforligochapterhrtsrequirement}. To determine the amount of isolation required, the typical motion of the optical table needs to be compared to the motion requirement for the optics; see references~\cite{HRTS_pre_concept,HAM_table_motion}. To passively isolate the optics, triple suspensions, known as HAM Relay-optics Triple Suspensions (HRTS), will be used.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:BHD_for_ligo_conclusion_1}
One of the major changes to LIGO for A+ will be the implementation of BHD because BHD is required for the LIGO detectors to meet the sensitivity requirement for A+ as frequency dependent squeezing requires the homodyne angle to be a free parameter. Details of the LO path were discussed and analysed. The LO will be derived from a beam picked off from the POP port, and a sketch of how the signal and LO will be combined is shown in Figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochaptersketchoflodelivery}. We derived the relative path length stability requirement for the LO and signal beams by; the requirement is shown in \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterhrtsrequirement}. The local oscillator's phase noise requirement led to HRTS being chosen to steer the LO and signal beams onto the combining beam splitter, BHDBS2. 
%\paragraph{}
%BHD presents some technical challenges compared to DC readout. The LO phase noise must be kept low enough as to not contaminate the signal at the AS port with LO path noise. The requirement for the phase noise is shown in Figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterhrtsrequirement}. This requirement can be met by using triple suspensions to deliver to LO and signal beams to the beam splitter they are combined on.
