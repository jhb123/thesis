\section{Local Oscillator Path Length Stability Requirement and HRTS requirement}
The requirement for the path length stability of the local oscillator is determined by the sensitivity goal for A+. Deconvolving the strain sensitivity curve for A+ with the linear response function between differential strain, $h$, and power at the AS port gives the noise of the AS beam. This is then converted to an equivalent phase noise between the AS the LO beams. Finally, the requirement for the suspended optics is derived.
\paragraph{}
A gravitational wave produces a phase modulated signal in the AS beam. The LO beam will also be phase modulated by the motion of the mirrors in the LO path. When these beams are combined, it is the phase difference between the two fields that produces a signal, so the phase noise of the LO path must be sufficiently low that the signal that is generated by the interference of the LO and AS beams corresponds to the phase modulation of the AS beam. 
\subsection{Local Oscillator Path}
Two options for the local oscillator path were considered: a beam picked off from the main beam splitters AR coating (BSAR) or the beam transmitted through PR2 \cite{Zhang2019} (POP). The determining factor in choice of LO path was the effect of backscatter, and the POP choice is far superior to the BSAR choice at all frequencies and scatter phases. This is because any backscatter from the BSAR enters only one arm, and therefore is a differential effect. Any back scatter that enters the POP port is common-mode, meaning that the differential arm signal is far less sensitive to this source of backscatter.

\subsection{Simulation of the Linear Response Function Between a Differential Strain and the Power at the AS Port}
The linear response function of the A+ detectors was obtained using \textsc{Finesse}. Since the core optics of the interferometer will not be changing in a way relevant to this calculation, the LLO model\cite{l1_finesse_2014} was used to calculate the response between differential motion of the ETMs and the power transmitted through the OMC. To apply a differential arm signal with \textsc{Finesse}, \verb|fsig| commands were used to apply two signals in antiphase with each other to the ETMs,
\begin{verbatim}
fsig sig1 ETMXHR 1 0
fsig sig2 ETMYHR 1 180.
\end{verbatim}
The frequency of this modulation was swept, and the signal produced at the AS port was measured by demodulating the light at the modulation frequency. The commands
\begin{verbatim}
xaxis sig1 f log 5 5000 2999
put P_DC_OMC_probe f1 $x1
\end{verbatim}
do this. Since the input modulation is doubled by moving the mirrors differentially the response needs dividing by 2. To account for the ETMs retro-reflecting the light (\textcolor{red}{or is the weird Finesse convention}), the response needs dividing by 2 again. To convert this from phase units to strain units, a factor of $\frac{\lambda}{2\pi L_{\rm{arm}}}$ is needed. Therefore, the output of the the \textsc{Finesse} simulation needs to be multiplied by $\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}\frac{2\pi L_{\rm{arm}}}{\lambda}$ to be in units of $\frac{\rm{W}}{\rm{h}}$. The linear response function between a differential arm strain and the power at the AS port is shown in figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterdetectorrepsonse}.

\subsection{Converting the Sensitivity Curve for A+ into the HRTS requirement}
\textcolor{red}{The sensitivity curve will be shown in an earlier chapter.}To convert the A+ sensitivity curve into a path length stability requirement for the local oscillator, a few steps are required. First, the sensitivity curve must be expressed in units of $\frac{\rm{W}}{\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}}$. Secondly, the intensity noise of the AS beam needs to be converted into a phase noise. In order for the phase noise of the local oscillator to not limit the performance of the gravitational wave detector, the phase noise of the AS beam must be greater than the phase noise of the LO beam. Finally, a factor of 10 safety margin is required. The HRTS requirement is then obtained by including the number of reflective optics in the LO path.
\paragraph{}
The A+ sensitivity\cite{a+curve} curve was multiplied by the function shown in \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterdetectorrepsonse} to convert it from units of $\frac{\rm{h}}{\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}}$ to $\frac{\rm{W}}{\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}}$. This is shown in figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterlightnoise}. At frequencies above 1\,kHz, the requirement is flat as this is the region dominated by shot noise. At lower frequencies, the requirement is relaxed as the sensitivity curve becomes dominated by other noise sources.

\paragraph{}
The quantum mechanics of this problem have been included in the A+ sensitivity curve \cite{a+curve}, therefore we can approach this with classical physics. When the LO and AS beams interfere at the beam splitter (BHSBS2), the power of the resulting beam is 
\begin{equation}
P = |E_s e^{i\omega t} + E_{\rm{LO}} e^{i\omega t + \phi}  |^2 = |E_s|^2 + |E_{\rm{LO}}|^2 + 2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\cos(\phi), 
\end{equation}
where $E_s$ and $E_{\rm{LO}}$ are the (complex) field amplitude of the signal and local oscillator beams, $\omega$ is the laser frequency, $t$ is time, and $\phi$ is the phase difference between the local oscillator and the signal beam, also known as the homodyne angle. Differentiating with respect to $\phi$ gives the relationship between a change in power and a change in phase between the two beams,
\begin{equation}
dP = -2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|\sin(\phi) d\phi.
\end{equation}
and since the homodyne angle is 90\si{\degree}, this becomes
\begin{equation}
dP = -2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{LO}}|d\phi.
\end{equation}
To convert $\phi$ to distance, use
\begin{equation}
\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}d\phi = dx.
\end{equation}
The light noise curve shown in figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterlightnoise} should be multiplied by  $\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2|E_sE^\dagger_{\rm{sig}}|}$ to obtain the the noise floor of the interferometer in units of LO path length noise. For a 98\,mW LO beam and a 5\,mW AS beam, the noise floor of the interferometer in units of LO path length is shown in figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterhrtsrequirement}.
\paragraph{}
The HRTS requirement assumes, based on the current design, that there will be 2 mirrors at 45\si{\degree}  which are used to steer the AS and LO beams. As the optics reflected at 45 degrees, the path length change due to the optics motion is multiplied by$\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}$. The beams are combined on a beamsplitter, and the notion of this beam splitter needs to be counted twice. A sketch of this is shown in figure \ref{fig:bhdforligochapterbhddoublecounted}. A factor of 10 is then included as a safety margin since there are many uncorrelated noise sources in LIGO. With a 98\,mW LO beam and a 5\,mW AS beam, the requirement for the HRTS optics longitudinal motion at 100\,Hz is $5\times10^{-17} \rm{m}/\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}$.
A typical motion of the optical benches at 10\,Hz is $10\times10^{-1}\rm{m}/\sqrt{\rm{Hz}}$ \cite{HAM_table_motion}. To achieve this with passive isolation, a triple suspensions will be used.
