Towards the end of the 19th century, it was becoming clear that Newton's theory of gravity and Maxwell's theory of electromagnetism were not in agreement; the force law arising from Maxwell's equations that governs the motion of charged particles in magnetic fields did not have an absolute frame of reference and the mechanics of the situation follow Lorentz transformations; in contrast, Newton's laws of motion dictate that objects obey Galilean transformations. 
\paragraph{}
In 1915, Einstein resolved this contradiction by formulating special relativity. This naturally lead to him establishing a general theory of relativity. This theory explained the long-standing mystery surrounding the precession of the perihelion of Mercury's orbit. It predicted the the deflection of light in a gravitational field and the dilation caused gravitational fields. Measurements verifying these predictions were made during the 20th century.
\paragraph{}
The final prediction of general relativity to be measured was gravitational waves. These signals are very weak even in the most extreme of environments, such as the coalescing of black holes. The existence of these could be infered from the discovery of a pulsar signal by Taylor and Hulse, for which they won the 1995 Nobel prize. It was not until 2015 that LIGO directly measured a signal generated by the merging of two black holes that the existence gravitational waves was finally confirmed, and all the predictions of Einstein's theory were measured.
\paragraph{}
However, the story of gravitational wave detectors is far from over. Current observatories are reaching their design sensitivities, and soon gravitational wave detections will become routine. These will give astronomers large datasets with which they can develop our understanding of how our universe is structured.
\paragraph{}
In the coming decades, new observatories capable of probing to larger red shifts will be constructed. With these, our understanding of physics may be revolutionised. The theory of general relativity does not translate into the quantum world, there is still mystery surrounding dark matter and dark energy and the earliest movements of our universe after the big bang cannot be studied with current tools. 
\paragraph{}
With every new method of observing the universe, new phenomena have been discovered. Telescopes allowed astronomers to study planets and stars, and radio telescopes revolutionised our understanding of cosmology. Gravitational waves have given us a new way of probing the universe and detecting the existence of objects that are not predicted by our current model of physics.
